# Verified Date Grammar — CRM Data Services

Generated by `grammar/probe.py` against the real service code in `code/old/`,
with watsonx/Presto stubbed. Every row below is an executed result, not a
reading of the regex.

Reference date for all probes: **2026-08-26**. Fiscal year = Apr–Mar.

---

## 1. Blocking bug — `event_report.py` is down for all dated queries

`EventIntentDetector.detect_date_intent` ([event_report.py:1780](../../code/old/event_report.py#L1780))
references `is_qoq` at lines **1829** and **2002**, but never assigns it. The
name is only bound inside two unrelated module-level functions (lines 681, 766),
which are a different scope.

`lead_report.py:2844` contains exactly the assignment that is missing:

```python
is_qoq = any(k in q for k in ["qoq","quarter over quarter","quarter-on-quarter",
                             "quarterly","quarter wise","quarter on quarter"])
```

`lead`, `task` and `opportunity` each have 9 occurrences of `is_qoq`; `event` has 6.

**Effect.** The `NameError` propagates through `process()` (uncaught at
[event_report.py:2714](../../code/old/event_report.py#L2714)) to the endpoint
handler at line 2956, which converts it to **HTTP 500**.

Measured on real UAT prompts: **8 of 12 fail**. The only survivors are queries
with no date reference at all.

| Query | Result |
|---|---|
| `total events for April, May and June 2026` | HTTP 500 |
| `total appointments scheduled between 1 April 2026 and 30 June 2026` | HTTP 500 |
| `show me total meetings booked in last quarter` | HTTP 500 |
| `show me total events in fy 2024` | HTTP 500 |
| `total events` (no date) | works |

**This is a one-line fix and it must land before any normalisation work is
measurable on the Event tool.**

## 2. Canonical date forms — what actually parses

Target: `1 April 2026 → 30 June 2026`. `OK` = exact match.

| Form emitted | lead | opp | task | case | targets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `between 1 April 2026 and 30 June 2026` | OK | OK | OK | **1-day collapse** | **1-day collapse** |
| **`1 April 2026 to 30 June 2026`** | **OK** | **OK** | **OK** | **OK** | wrong |
| `from 1 April 2026 to 30 June 2026` | OK | OK | OK | **ends today** | wrong |
| `01-04-2026 to 30-06-2026` | **full FY** | **full FY** | **full FY** | OK | **full FY** |
| **`April, May and June 2026`** | **OK** | **OK** | **OK** | **OK** | April only |
| `April, May, June 2026` (no `and`) | OK | OK | OK | OK | wrong |
| `April 2026, May 2026 and June 2026` | **drops April** | **drops April** | **drops April** | OK | **drops April** |

### Rules this yields

**EMIT:**
- Explicit range → `1 April 2026 to 30 June 2026` — bare `to`, spelled-out month.
  The only form correct across lead, opp, task and case.
- Month list → `April, May and June 2026` — year stated **once**, at the end.

**NEVER EMIT:**
- `between X and Y` — collapses to a single day in `case` and `targets`.
- `from X to Y` — in `case`, the end date becomes *today*.
- `dd-mm-yyyy` numerics — silently widened to the full FY in four of five services.
- Year repeated per month (`April 2026, May 2026 and June 2026`) — **silently drops
  the first month** in four of five services. This form appears in ~15 UAT prompts
  (#17–#23, #42–#48), all currently under-counting by one month.

## 3. Comparison qualifiers discard the date range

With a qualifier present, the explicit window is thrown away entirely.

Query: `total leads <qualifier> 1 April 2026 to 30 June 2026`

| Qualifier | lead | opp | task |
|---|---|---|---|
| `qoq` | all 4 quarters of FY2026 | all 4 quarters FY2026 | all 4 quarters FY2026 |
| `yoy` | **FY2020**→FY2026 | **FY2018**→FY2026 | **FY2020**→FY2026 |

Two separate defects:

1. **The requested window is ignored.** The user asks for Apr–Jun 2026; they
   receive the whole FY, or nine fiscal years.
2. **YoY has a hardcoded start year that differs per service** — 2020 in lead and
   task, 2018 in opportunity. The same question against two tools returns
   different spans.

Affects UAT prompts #29, #30, #90, #101, #102 and ~15 others.

`month on month` / `mom` / `monthly` / `month wise` all behave identically once
the bare-`to` range form is used — hyphenation only matters in the month-list form.

`case` ignores comparison qualifiers in its date parser altogether (returns a
plain tuple); its grouping is decided elsewhere.

**Normaliser rule:** a comparison qualifier combined with an explicit sub-year
range is a **semantic conflict**. Resolve it explicitly — either drop the
qualifier and honour the window, or expand the window to match the qualifier —
rather than letting each backend pick silently and differently.

## 4. `targetvsactuals.py` cannot parse ranges at all

**1 of 21** asserted variants resolved correctly — only `single month + year`.

| Input | Returns |
|---|---|
| `between 1 April 2026 and 30 June 2026` | `2026-04-01 → 2026-04-01` (one day) |
| `1 April 2026 to 30 June 2026` | `2026-06-26 → 2026-06-30` |
| `from 1 April 2026 to 30 June 2026` | `2026-06-26 → 2026-06-30` |
| `01-04-2026 to 30-06-2026` | `2026-04-01 → 2027-03-31` (full FY) |
| `April, May and June 2026` | April only |

No emittable canonical form exists for this service. It needs a parser fix, not
a normalisation workaround. Until then, any date-scoped targets-vs-actuals

## 5. Fiscal vs calendar quarters — a semantic mismatch, not a bug

All five services agree: `Q2 2026` → **1 Jul – 30 Sep 2026**. That matches
`behavior.md:429` (`Q2 = July 1 → September 30`), so the code is internally
consistent and correct against its own spec.

However, UAT prompt #131 reads:

> `Total opportunity for eden in Q2 2026 (April to June)`

The client's gloss defines Q2 as **April–June** — calendar Q2. The system returns
**July–September** — fiscal Q2.

Every `Q<n>` query is therefore off by one quarter from the user's expectation,
silently and consistently. This is a **product decision**, not a defect: either
re-label the fiscal quarters in the UI, or accept calendar quarters and remap.
It must be settled before "Q2" can be normalised, because the correct output
depends entirely on which definition is authoritative.

## 6. `case_report` discards the year on any multi-month range

The most damaging defect found, because it returns plausible numbers for the
wrong year rather than failing.

`get_date_range_for_query` calls `extract_specific_months_from_query`
([case_report.py:273](../../code/old/case_report.py#L273)), which fires whenever
**two or more month names** appear. It keeps only the month *numbers* and
substitutes the **current** FY, discarding the year the user wrote. Its own log:

```
extract_specific_months_from_query: Found 2 unique months: [4, 6]
Specific months [4, 6] detected for FY 2026-2027: 2026-04-01 to 2026-06-30
```

| Query to case_report | Returns | Correct? |
|---|---|---|
| `1 April 2024 to 30 June 2024` | 2026-04-01 → 2026-06-30 | **no — year replaced** |
| `April, May and June 2024` | 2026-04-01 → 2026-06-30 | **no — year replaced** |
| `15 April 2024 to 28 September 2025` | 2026-04-01 → 2026-09-30 | **no — both years replaced** |
| `1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026` | 2027-03-01 → 2026-04-30 | **no — inverted, returns 0 rows** |
| `June 2024` | 2024-06-01 → 2024-06-30 | yes |
| `fy 2024` | 2024-04-01 → 2025-03-31 | yes |
| `last fy` | 2025-04-01 → 2026-03-31 | yes |

A whole-FY range is not merely wrong but **inverted** — start after end. In SQL
`BETWEEN` that matches nothing, so historical case queries return empty.

Earlier probes missed this because they targeted 2026, which happens to be the
current FY, so the substituted year coincided with the expected one. Any probe
of date handling must use a **past** year.

### Rules this yields

- A whole financial year is emitted as **`fy <year>`** for every tool. Verified
  correct on lead, opp, task and case.
- A whole-month range **within one calendar year** is emitted as a month-name
  range with the year once at the end — **`April to June 2024`** — which case
  parses correctly (measured 26 Aug 2026: `('2024-04-01','2024-06-30')`). The
  substitution bug fires on the **day form** and the **month list** form, not on
  this one. Cross-calendar-year name ranges invert
  (`November 2024 to February 2025` → 2027-02-01 → 2026-11-30), so they are
  never emitted.
- Month **lists** are still **decomposed into one call per month** for case.
- An arbitrary mid-month day range (`15 April 2024 to 28 September 2025`) has
  **no safe textual form** for case. The normaliser emits a warning; the real
  fix is resolved dates as parameters.

## 7. Per-year series (yearly breakdowns) — measured 26 Aug 2026

Found via a production failure: `total leads FY2019-20 to FY2026-27 yearly`
collapsed to a single FY2019 call, and the master agent then looped over the
years by hand, lost half of them, and invented explanations for the holes.

What each parser does with the candidate forms (today = FY2026):

| Form | lead | opp | task | event | case |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `yoy` / `yearly` / `year wise` (no years) | FY2020→26, one row per FY | FY2018→26 | FY2020→26 | all years, no date filter | **one wrong range 2022→2027** |
| bare `2019 to 2026` | **exact span, one row per FY** | same | same | **crash** (is_qoq) | current FY only |
| `yoy 2020 to 2026` | floor→current (range ignored) | **FY2018→26 — range ignored** | floor→current | **exact span, one row per FY** | wrong range |
| `fy 2019 to fy 2026` | keeps only 2019 & 2026 | keeps only 2019 | keeps only 2019 & 2026 | crash | keeps only 2019 |
| `FY2019-20 to FY2026-27` | keeps only FY2019 | keeps only FY2019 | parses nothing | crash | keeps only FY2019 |

**EMIT** for a year series:
- lead / opp / task → bare **`2020 to 2026`** (exact span honoured; no yoy token,
  which would swap the span for the hardcoded floor)
- event → **`yoy 2020 to 2026`** (the yoy branch sits before the crash line and,
  uniquely in event, honours the range)
- case / targets → **one `fy <year>` call per year** (no series support at all)

**NEVER EMIT:** `fy A to fy B`, `FY2019-20 to FY2026-27`, or a `yoy` token on a
lead/opp/task year range.

A month or quarter series across several years (`qoq last 3 years`) has the
same trap: `qoq 2023 to 2025` collapses to one FY. **`qoq fy 2023`** /
**`mom fy 2023`** are verified (4 quarters / 12 months of exactly that FY), so
the normaliser emits one per year.

A month series **within** a sub-year window keeps both the window and the grain
only in the month-name form: **`mom June to December 2025`** → month-wise rows
June–December in lead/opp/task, correct span in case. The day form next to any
comparison token (`mom 1 June 2025 to 31 December 2025`) makes the backends
discard the window and answer for the whole FY — never emit it. Where the
window cannot be expressed this way (mid-month days, cross-calendar-year), the
normaliser keeps the window and drops the token, with a warning.

## 8. The seven funnel services — measured 27 Aug 2026

The report tools were probed first; the funnels were not, because the
normaliser only routed to them. A 1,000-prompt batch forced the gap open.

**Probe the ENDPOINT path, not just the resolver.** `source_funnel`,
`lead_conversion_funnel`, `lead_user_funnel` and `sales_user_funnel` all share
a `DateResolver`, but their endpoints intercept mom/qoq/yoy *before* the
resolver runs. Probing `DateResolver.resolve()` alone reported "grain
dropped" for all four; probing `_mom_periods` / `_qoq_periods` — what the
endpoint actually calls — showed correct 12-month and 4-quarter series. One
wrong layer produced four false findings.

**Probe with a PAST year — again.** `q1 2026` looked correct in every service.
With `q1 2024` the split appeared: four services silently return the *current*
FY's quarter.

| Form | project | product | subsource | source / lead / lead-user / sales-user |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fy 2024` | ok | ok | ok | ok |
| `August 2024` | ok | ok | ok | ok |
| `q1 2024` (bare year) | ok | **ok** | ok | **current FY — wrong year** |
| `q1 fy 2024` | ok | **whole FY** | ok | **ok** |
| `q4 <any form>` | ok | ok | ok | **current FY — wrong year** |
| `mom fy 2024` / `qoq fy 2024` | ok | **grain dropped** | ok | ok (endpoint) |
| `1 Jul 2024 to 30 Sep 2024` | ok | ok | **tail of end month** | user funnels: **tail of end month**; source/lead: ok |
| `last 15 days` | ok | ok | ok (own convention, ±2 days) | ok |

### Rules this yields

- Whole quarter → **`q<n> fy <year>`** everywhere except `product_funnel`,
  which needs the bare **`q<n> <year>`**.
- **Q4** has no safe single-call form in the DateResolver family: source and
  lead fall through to the day form (verified there), and the two user funnels
  decompose into three month calls.
- **product_funnel** cannot express a mom/qoq series over a financial year in
  any phrasing (`mom April to March 2025` resolves to FY2025-26), so product
  grain decomposes into one single-period call per month or quarter.
- A rolling window ending today on a service that collapses day ranges is
  emitted as **`last <n> days`** — the service applies its own day convention,
  so the agent must label from `returned_period`.
- No funnel service has a per-year series form; year series decompose into one
  `fy <year>` call each.

## 9. Weeks, days and the completed-period rule — 31 August 2026

**"Last N units" means N COMPLETED units. The current, part-finished one is
excluded.** On 31 August, "last 30 days" is 1–30 August, not 2–31 August.
Client-stated, and it matches the backends: `lead_report.py:2961` sets
`end = today - 1` before counting back.

The month, quarter and year branches always worked this way. **Days and weeks
did not** — they counted back from `today` and included it, so every such
window sat one day forward of both the client's definition and the backend's.
Silent, and invisible in a round-trip test, because an explicit day-form range
makes the backend use whatever dates it is handed.

**"This week" did not exist at all.** It matched no branch and fell through to
the current-financial-year default: a twelve-month answer to a seven-day
question, with no warning. `today` and `yesterday` were likewise missing from
the multi-period phrase list, so "today vs yesterday" collapsed to one call.

Also measured: the words `today` and `yesterday` return **no date intent** in
lead, opp and task, and the **current FY** in case. Never emit them — a single
day is emitted as `31 August 2026 to 31 August 2026`, which is exact
everywhere.

### Rolling windows are per-service, and all three disagree

For a window of *n* days ending yesterday:

| Service | What `last n days` returns | Emit |
|---|---|---|
| lead / opp / task / source / project / product / lead funnel | — (day form parses exactly) | day form |
| `case_report` | today−(n−1) … **today** | `last n+1 days`, warn: one day wider |
| `subsource_funnel` | yesterday−n … yesterday | `last n−1 days` — **exact** |
| `lead_user` / `sales_user` | today−(n−1) … **today** | `last n+1 days`, warn: one day wider |

`case_report` cannot express *any* sub-month day range: `24 August 2026 to
30 August 2026` collapses to the 24th alone, because its single
`<day> <month>` branch matches first. Its `last week` is exact, but its
**`this week` runs Monday to Sunday — six days into the future** — so that
phrase is never emitted; the rolling form covers it instead.

Where no exact form exists, the rule is to emit the form that **covers** the
requested span, warn, and let the agent label from `returned_period`. A window
one day wide of the request, declared, beats a window silently collapsed to a
single day.

## 10. Funnel breakdown must survive into the canonical text — 2 Sep 2026

Routing and wording are two separate things, and getting the first right is
not enough.

`product funnel for June 2026` routed correctly to `product_funnel`, but the
emitted text was a bare **`funnel June 2026`**. The breakdown word was gone,
because `find_groupings` matches `product wise` and not `product funnel`, so
`groupings` came back empty and `render_query` had nothing to append. The
collaborator then read a plain funnel request and ran the overall lead
conversion funnel instead, returning one all-products row under a heading
saying Product Funnel.

`resolve_funnel_tool` now returns the facet a bare noun form implies, and the
normaliser adds it to the groupings, so the text reads
`funnel product wise June 2026`. The same applies to source, sub-source,
project and the two user funnels.

**The grouping is implied only when no value is named on that facet.**
`funnel for Eden June 2026` stays `funnel for EDEN June 2026`: Eden is a
filter, and adding a product grouping would strip it, turning one product's
funnel into every product's.

A second guard came out of the same fix. The unknown-entity check read
`funnel by source June 2026` as naming a source called June, because a
capitalised word after a facet noun looked like a value. Month names, day
names, period words and bare numbers are now excluded from that check.

## 11. `last N fy` matched nothing and defaulted to the current year — 8 Sep 2026

Reported as "last fy normalises to the wrong year". Singular `last fy` was
always right (`fy 2025` = 1 Apr 2025 → 31 Mar 2026, `period_display`
`FY2025-26`). The plural was not:

| query | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `last 2 years` | 1 Apr 2024 → 31 Mar 2026 | unchanged |
| `last 2 fy` | **1 Apr 2026 → 31 Mar 2027** | 1 Apr 2024 → 31 Mar 2026 |
| `last 2 financial years` | **1 Apr 2026 → 31 Mar 2027** | 1 Apr 2024 → 31 Mar 2026 |
| `last 3 fy` | **1 Apr 2026 → 31 Mar 2027** | 1 Apr 2023 → 31 Mar 2026 |

Two causes, both one line:

1. The `last N <unit>` alternation listed `year` but not `fy`, so `last 2 fy`
   matched no branch in `resolve` at all.
2. `_clean` collapsed `financial year` → `fy` without the plural, so
   `last 2 financial years` never even reached that alternation.

**The failure mode is what matters.** Neither miss raised anything. The query
fell past every branch to the no-period default, which is the *current*
financial year — so a request for the last two years was answered with a
partial current year, under a heading naming a period the user never asked
for. A date branch that silently defaults is worse than one that errors:
every future `last N <newunit>` addition must be probed for this.

**`this quarter` and `last fy` were both correct** when checked on
2026-09-08: `this quarter` → 1 Jul → 30 Sep 2026 (fiscal Q2, §5), `last fy` →
`fy 2025` / FY2025-26. The reported symptom was the *display* of `fy 2025` as
"FY 2024-2025" by the agent reading the canonical text instead of
`period_display` — a behaviour-layer fix, now §4.3a of the master behaviour.

### The general fix: an unresolvable `last N <unit>` now asks

Probing the rest of the family found three more of the same silent default —
`last 3 wks`, `last 3 dys` and `last 3 fin years` all returned the current
financial year. Those three are now aliases (`wks`/`dys` in `_clean`
alongside the existing `qtrs`/`yrs`/`mnths`, and `fin year` joining
`financial year`).

Aliases alone only fix the spellings someone thought of. So `resolve` now
checks, immediately before the no-period default, for a `last N <time unit>`
phrase it did not handle, and returns `UNRESOLVED` — which the normaliser
turns into "I could not determine a date range from that". `last 3
fortnights`, `last 2 semesters`, `last 3 mons` and `last 3 mondays` ask
instead of answering the wrong period.

The guard is deliberately restricted to words that really are time units, so
`last 5 leads` — a count, not a period — keeps the documented current-FY
default.

**The principle:** in a date grammar, a branch that falls through to a
default is more dangerous than one that raises. The default is plausible, it
is never questioned, and its warning ("no date expression found") is a lie
whenever the query plainly contained one. Prefer asking.

### Related: a leading "this" was read as anaphora

`^\s*(it|this|that|these|those|they)\b` classified `this month sales`,
`this quarter leads` and `this fy leads` as follow-up fragments needing prior
context, and refused them. `this` before a period word names the period, not
something said earlier; the other pronouns keep the plain rule.
